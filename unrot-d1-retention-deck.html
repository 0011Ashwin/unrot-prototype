01 · The bet

D1 is won where session one ends.

Every new user walks away with a finished lesson and no promise of tomorrow. We add one hand-off at the end of the first session — a concrete “tomorrow’s five minutes” plan, a reminder at the hour they choose, and a day-2 card waiting for them. That’s the gap between 16% and 22%.

6 percentage points but ~37% more of the people who tried us. That is not a content problem — it’s a returns-problem: nothing in the product gives a user a reason to come back within 24 hours.

02 · The problem

The day-2 return is decided at the end of day 1.

D1 is the share of new users who return the calendar day after their first session. The decision to come back is made while session 1 is still open — then the app goes silent for 24 hours. The few products that hit 22% don’t rely on goodwill; they plant the reason to return before the user leaves.

03 · Why they don’t return

Four testable hypotheses — all live at the end of session 1.

H1 · No hand-off

Done means done

The lesson ends and so does the session. No screen ever says “here’s what’s next.”

H2 · No stake

No commitment

The user hasn’t set a goal or a time slot, so nothing anchors the return ahead of the next session.

H3 · No nudge

Quiet 24 hours

There’s no prompt when the user has a moment — the day passes in silence.

H4 · No progress

Nothing carried

Finished-and-forgotten: no saved state makes day 2 worth starting (a review deck, a streak, a plan).

Each hypothesis maps to a cheap, independently-measurable intervention. We pick the two that compound: the hand-off and the commitment behind it (H1 + H2), delivered as one redesigned final screen — because building a hook without a reason-to-return is just decoration, and a reason without a hook never gets seen.

04 · The solution

“Tomorrow’s five minutes.” A hand-off, not a gimmick.

We change what session 1 ends with. Instead of “thanks for learning,” the user gets a plan card: what they learned today, what tomorrow’s 5-minute lesson is, and a reminder at the hour they choose. One loop, owned end to end.

Session 1 ends

“You learned 3 terms.”

Progress is made visible and concrete — not a score, the actual knowledge.

The plan card

“Tomorrow: Prompting basics.”

Tomorrow named + “remind me at 7:45am.” The slot is the commitment.

The reminder

“Your 5 min are ready.”

Email-first for speed and reliability; push slides in where permissions exist.

Day 2 opens

Back, with a plan.

The card opens the promised 5-minute lesson; the chain can repeat tomorrow.

Four weeks, one loop, cadence with room.

AnalytDesign

Add events: session1_complete, reminder_accept, day2_start. Pull the session-1 funnel to confirm where users drop. Designer locks hi-fi flows for the hand-off and day-2 landing; copy written and tested on 5 users.

Week 2Build
EngDesign

Build the plan card + slot capture behind a feature flag. Ship to an internal beta on the final day. Funnel check: does the extra screen dent session-1 completion? (Guardrail metric live.)

Week 3Deliver
Eng

Scheduler + email-first reminder; day-2 landing page. QA the calendar-day definition and timezones — D1 is per-user local day, so this is where silent bugs hide. Push delivery wired but gated off.

Week 4Launch
AnalytEng

50/50 randomized rollout on all new users, code frozen. First D1 readout lands the day after the early cohort’s day-2 — a directional read within the window, full power as cohorts accumulate.

Roles: the designer front-loads (weeks 1–2), both engineers carry weeks 2–4. Buffer kept for reminder deliverability — the top failure risk — with email-first as the fallback that needs no permission capture at all.

09 · The ask

Ship the hand-off. Learn from the return.

Six points separate us from the benchmark, and none of them require new content — they require treating the end of session 1 as a decision point instead of a goodbye.

The solution

Re-architect session 1’s ending around “tomorrow’s five minutes”: a learned-today recap, a named tomorrow, a chosen reminder slot, and a day-2 card that opens the promised lesson. Email-first to de-risk deliverability; push and streaks as the next quarter’s compounding layer.

The proof

Pre-registered 50/50 experiment on new-user D1 live by week 4. Guardrails protect session 1. We’ll know within days of launch whether the hand-off moves the number — and the funnel events tell us exactly where to tune next.
